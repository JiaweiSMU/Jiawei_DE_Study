### Articulating thoughts clearly
Being articulate means delivering ideas fluently and coherently (clear and concise).

**Deciding what to say**
- Know the topic well
- Define the goal of the talk
- Find the fastest path to explain it
- Anticipate questions and answer them upfront
**Delivery**
- Lead with the main idea (top-down)
- Cut flowery language
- Show with specifics instead of telling
### Interview prep
1. **Study the interview structure** to learn which question types to expect.
2. **Think from first principles:** break the problem into basic components, explain each, then push one step further.
3. **Take the interviewer's point of view:** by trying to ask what do they think about your approach
### Behavioural interviews
[[Behavioural Interview Dump]]
Each question tests a quality:
- **Leadership:** how proactive you are
- **Resilience:** how you respond to challenges or failure (e.g. "proudest accomplishment" should show an obstacle overcome)
- **Teamwork**
- **Influence / persuasion**
- **Ethical / moral conflicts**
**Preparation**
1. Brain-dump experiences that demonstrate each quality.
2. Pick at least 2 stories per quality.
3. Shape each story as STAR:
    - **Situation:** 2–3 sentences of context
    - **Task:** what you were asked to do
    - **Action:** what you did, including trade-offs considered
    - **Result:** the outcome and what you learned
Show, don't tell: don't claim you have leadership; tell the story that demonstrates it.
### About me answer
- Keep it under 2 minutes.
- Stay high-level but specific, giving enough to invite follow-up questions.
- Tell a cohesive story from your resume: the 2–3 most relevant points, logically linked.
- End with why you're excited about the role.
**Simple structure:** where I'm from → education → 2–3 linked experiences, each leading to the next (what I did → what I enjoyed → why it led me here) → why I'm excited.
